Not a radio this time. A map.

I wanted to know who actually holds the land around here, so I pulled the public
records together into one map you can poke at. The headline surprised me: about
**65%** of San Diego County is public, tribal or conserved land. That's roughly
1.76 million of the county's 2.69 million acres.

**[Open the San Diego County land map →]({{ '/maps/san-diego-land-holders/' | relative_url }})**

## What's on it

Each holder is its own layer, and you can switch them on and off from the
ledger on the left. Click any parcel for details.

| Holder | Acres | Share of county |
|---|---:|---:|
| State parks & wildlife (Anza-Borrego, Cuyamaca, reserves) | 664k | 24.7% |
| National forest (Cleveland) | 289k | 10.7% |
| BLM | 191k | 7.1% |
| Military (Pendleton, Miramar, Navy bases) | 174k | 6.5% |
| Tribal (18 reservations) | 133k | 4.9% |
| Cities | 84k | 3.1% |
| Water & power (reservoirs, water districts, SDG&E) | 79k | 2.9% |

Anza-Borrego alone does a lot of the lifting in that top row.

There are overlays, too: the Spanish and Mexican **ranchos** from before 1848,
37 leftover **State Lands Commission** school-land squares and tidelands
(2,641 acres between them), some notable private holdings like Guejito and
Palomar, special designations, a satellite layer from USGS, and the county's
**shooting restrictions**. That last one is a picture of where county code bans
or limits firearms. It is not permission to shoot anywhere.

## The fine print

Boundaries come from the source parcels and are simplified only until the
enclosed area stops changing, so the shapes stay close to the records they came
from. Acreages are still approximate. Each acre is counted once, so overlapping
claims don't double up. Road and utility rights-of-way and easements aren't
shown.

## Where the data came from

None of this is my survey work. It's stitched together from:

- the [California Protected Areas Database](https://www.calands.org/cpad/)
  (CPAD 2026a, GreenInfo Network)
- SANDAG public land ownership (from assessor records) and SANDAG's ranchos layer
- U.S. Census TIGERweb, for tribal and military boundaries
- the San Diego County Sheriff, for the shooting zones
- USGS, for the imagery

Thanks to all of them for putting it out in the open.
